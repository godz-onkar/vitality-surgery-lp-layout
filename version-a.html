The usual route to knee or shoulder surgery goes like this.

  • Weeks to get an appointment, then more weeks to get a surgery date.
  • A short visit that leaves you with more questions than you came in with.
  • A deductible, coinsurance and bills that arrive months later.
  • Calls with the insurance company about what’s covered.
  • A recovery plan that amounts to a printed handout.
  • No clear picture of when you’ll be back at work, or what week six should feel like.

Insurance decides what it will pay for. Dr. Ziegler decides what your knee or shoulder needs.

What surgery with Dr. Ziegler looks like.

He operates on knees and shoulders: knee arthroscopy (a scope) for meniscus and cartilage, shoulder arthroscopy and rotator cuff repair. He also repairs ACL, labral, biceps and triceps tears.

  • Before surgery day, he plans where you’ll sleep (often a recliner, after a rotator cuff repair), a protein target and your therapy schedule.
  • After surgery, he coaches your exercises himself and works directly with your therapist. At each follow-up, he tells you when it’s safe to drive, work and get back on the course or the court.
  • In his office, he can add hyperbaric oxygen, orthopedic laser and red light therapy to your recovery, and regenerative treatment when it fits.
  • One flat price covers the surgeon, the surgery center and anesthesia.

Questions people ask before they book

Does Dr. Ziegler take insurance?

For some commercial plans, yes, but insurance visits are limited to 30 minutes and one joint. Self-pay gives you a longer evaluation and nothing billed to insurance. It often suits people with high-deductible or out-of-network plans.

How much does surgery cost?

It depends on your procedure. Dr. Ziegler goes over the cost with you at your evaluation, because he’s the one examining your knee or shoulder and deciding what it needs. You get your price in writing before you commit.

How do I pay?

By credit card, debit card, HSA or FSA card, or through an approved financing partner. Payment is made in full before your surgery.

How long is recovery?

At your evaluation, Dr. Ziegler walks you through recovery for your procedure, including how much time you’ll need away from work.

Can I come for a second opinion?

Yes. Bring the other surgeon’s recommendation. Dr. Ziegler will examine you, go over your imaging and tell you what he’d do, even if that means waiting or trying non-surgical care first.

Do I need an MRI first?

No. If you have one, bring it. If you need one, Dr. Ziegler will tell you.
